General ledger, posting architecture and recognition

By the end, you can

  • Configure G/L setup, accounting periods, payment terms/methods, currencies and deferral schedules.
  • Design posting accounts, categories, subcategories, financial reports and G/L allocations.
  • Configure general/specific/inventory posting groups and controlled alternatives.

Translate policy into a chart and posting model

Begin with the accounting policy rather than a list of account numbers. Separate assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses, then decide which accounts accept postings and which organise totals. Account categories and subcategories support a consistent reporting structure. A financial report combines row definitions and column logic with dates, dimensions and other filters. A plausible total can still be wrong because a row range omits an account or the column compares the wrong period.

Northstar buys office equipment for resale and also purchases equipment for internal use. Both may come from the same vendor, but their business purpose differs. Document the transaction type before selecting accounts. For allocations, define an explainable driver such as floor area or a fixed agreed percentage. A 2,000 shared expense allocated 60% to SALES and 40% to ADMIN produces 1,200 and 800. Confirm that allocation totals preserve the original amount and that dimensions identify the intended recipients.

Distinguish posting groups by the question they answer

General business posting groups classify the trading party. General product posting groups classify what is sold or purchased. Their combination in General Posting Setup selects relevant sales and purchase accounts. Specific posting groups link a subledger type, such as a customer, vendor or bank, to its control accounts. Inventory Posting Setup combines inventory posting group and location to select inventory balance-sheet accounts. Tax posting uses its own setup and local rules.

Suppose customer C100 is DOMESTIC and item CHAIR has product group FURNITURE. The sales revenue account comes from that general business/product combination. The receivables account comes from the customer posting group. The inventory account follows the item’s inventory posting group and location. A missing revenue account is therefore not repaired by changing the customer’s receivables control account. Follow the error to the applicable setup matrix, correct the missing combination in the sandbox and preview posting again.

Where multiple customer or vendor posting groups are required, configure the supported allow-multiple controls and permitted alternatives, including the relevant master record. Do not tell users to choose any convenient control account. Test both allowed substitution and a prohibited alternative so the policy remains consistent across documents, journals and later application.

Dates, terms and methods solve different problems

Accounting periods define the financial calendar. Allowed posting dates limit when transactions may post and can be refined for individual users. Closing an accounting period or fiscal year is not the same control as preventing all posting to historical dates. Check both the calendar and the allowed-date configuration when diagnosing a rejected journal.

Payment terms determine due-date and discount-date behaviour. A payment method describes how settlement occurs and can carry balancing behaviour where configured. A customer’s 30-day terms do not identify the bank account that received the payment. Work date, document date, posting date and due date can differ. In scenario questions, write down which date drives the requirement before choosing a setup page. Avoid relying on the device’s current date in repeatable training exercises.

Recognition and foreign currency need explicit calculations

An annual service purchased for 12,000 at the start of a twelve-month period should recognise 1,000 per month in a simple equal-period example. Use an appropriate deferral template and inspect the generated schedule, dates, accounts and rounding before posting. The original invoice remains the commercial document; the deferral schedule controls recognition across periods. A prepayment secures part of a purchase or sale before final invoicing and is a different process.

For foreign currency, configure the currency and effective exchange rates, then distinguish transaction conversion from later revaluation. In a simplified example, a 1,000 foreign-currency payable initially converts at 18 local units per foreign unit, producing 18,000 LCY. At an approved closing rate of 18.5, the liability is 18,500 and the increase is 500. The sign and posting account depend on whether the exposure is an asset or liability and the configured gain/loss setup. Do not infer the direction from an ambiguous rate-entry convention: read the fields and verify the LCY result with a known amount.

Prove configuration using traceable posting preview

Prepare one small sales transaction, one purchase and one deferral. Before committing, use posting preview to identify the proposed G/L accounts, subledgers, tax treatment and dimensions. Compare these entries with a written expected-entry table. If the preview disagrees, inspect posting groups and dates before adjusting the document to hide the problem. In a real implementation, an authorised finance reviewer approves the accounting mapping. The course’s examples teach application reasoning and do not prescribe a company’s accounting policy.

Sandbox lab: Trace a sale, an annual expense and an allocation

Use synthetic records in your authorised sandbox. Record the company and work date before posting. Never run this exercise in a customer’s production company.

  1. Create a written account map in a disposable company. Mark posting accounts, categories and reporting groupings.
  2. Configure a customer, item and location using separate general, customer and inventory posting groups.
  3. Create a small sales document and use posting preview. Explain every proposed revenue, receivables and inventory account.
  4. Create a 12,000 annual expense using a twelve-period deferral schedule. Check total, monthly recognition and allowed posting dates.
  5. Prepare a 2,000 allocation with 60/40 recipients and verify 1,200 plus 800. Review dimensions.
  6. Configure a foreign currency and a known test rate. Verify a 1,000-unit transaction’s LCY conversion. Build a financial-report check for the affected accounts.

Expected observations

  • The account mapping explains the preview without confusing general and specific posting groups.
  • The deferral schedule totals 12,000 and the allocation totals 2,000.
  • The foreign-currency test converts to the independently calculated LCY amount.

Evidence to retain

  • Account mapping table
  • Posting-preview explanation
  • Deferral schedule and allocation calculations
  • Financial report filter and total checks

Cleanup

Delete unposted training documents and restore date restrictions. If posting was authorised, reverse through supported processes and preserve the audit trail rather than deleting ledger records.

Common pitfalls

  • Changing a receivables posting group to fix a missing revenue account.
  • Confusing payment terms with payment method or a deferral with a prepayment.
  • Assuming year-end closure alone blocks historical posting.

Check your understanding

1. A customer pays in 30 days by bank transfer. Which setup governs the due date?

Payment terms.

The payment method describes settlement; the terms determine due and applicable discount dates.

2. The sale’s revenue account is wrong but receivables is correct. Which combination should you inspect?

The general business and general product posting groups in General Posting Setup.

The customer posting group controls receivables, so changing it would target the wrong part of the entry.

3. A twelve-month 12,000 expense has twelve equal periods and no rounding complications. What is the expected monthly recognition?

1,000 per month.

Confirm both the total and period dates in the generated deferral schedule.
