Payments, reconciliation, foreign currency and period close

By the end, you can

  • Process payment/cash-receipt journals and Payment Registration; apply/unapply entries and reverse eligible journals.
  • Reconcile bank accounts, post recurring journals/allocations, adjust exchange rates, correct posted dimensions and revalue eligible G/L balances.
  • Process fixed-asset acquisition, depreciation and disposal with traceable ledger effects.

Settlement is more than entering a bank amount

A payment journal records outgoing settlements and a cash receipt journal records incoming receipts in the applicable workflow. Confirm account type, balancing bank, document type, currency, date and application before posting. Payment Registration can streamline supported customer receipts. In all cases, distinguish a posted payment from its application to open invoices and from its appearance on a bank statement.

Northstar owes a vendor 2,000 and has an approved credit memo for 300. Under a simple same-currency, no-discount example, the net settlement is 1,700. Apply the intended entries deliberately and verify remaining amounts after posting. If a receipt was applied to the wrong invoice, use the supported unapply process, inspect reversal of related application effects, then reapply correctly. Do not create a second bank receipt to repair an application mistake: that would duplicate cash.

Reconciliation explains the difference; it does not hide it

Bank reconciliation compares the bank statement with bank account ledger entries. Matching a statement line to an existing ledger entry avoids posting it twice. Unrecorded bank charges may require a supported journal/transfer process before reconciliation. Outstanding items explain timing differences. A zero difference is meaningful only when each match and adjustment has evidence.

Example: the bank statement ends at 9,850. The bank ledger shows 10,000 because a 150 fee is on the statement but not yet in Business Central. After reviewing the fee, post the approved charge through the bank-aware process and reconcile. If instead a 150 payment has already been posted and is simply unmatched, match it; posting another fee would be wrong. For automatic payment reconciliation, review proposed matches, ambiguous references and tolerances before posting. An amount match alone can connect the wrong invoice when two invoices share a value.

Recurring postings and reversals must preserve meaning

Review recurring journal dates, methods and allocation settings before each run. A reversal for an accrual creates the intended opposite effect in the appropriate period; it is not the same as deleting history. Supported Reverse Transaction actions have restrictions, including origin and application state. Inspect the source journal and related entries before choosing a correction path. Some transaction types require their own cancellation or credit process.

Use an allocation test whose driver is explicit. A 3,000 shared service allocated 50%, 30% and 20% produces 1,500, 900 and 600, preserving 3,000. Verify dimensions and recipients as well as the total. A balanced journal with swapped departments is still wrong. Retain the source policy, independent calculation and posting references.

Foreign currency and dimension corrections are distinct close tasks

Adjust exchange rates using the correct effective rates, posting date, scope and configured gain/loss accounts. Open customer/vendor balances and foreign-currency bank balances can have revaluation effects. A simplified 1,000 foreign-currency receivable moving from 18 to 18.5 LCY per unit increases its LCY value by 500. That illustrates exposure direction only; actual Business Central entries depend on its rate convention, rounding, application and setup. Inspect detailed ledger and G/L effects rather than changing the original invoice.

G/L balance revaluation is a separate supported process with its own eligibility and configuration. Do not assume that adjusting subledger exchange rates automatically meets every G/L revaluation requirement. Write the close checklist by account and process. For a posted department error on G/L entries, use the Dimension Correction Tool where appropriate. It corrects supported dimension analysis without being a general edit of the transaction’s amount or posting account. Review scope, blocked dimensions, validation and analysis-view updates. Do not promise that changing a G/L dimension automatically rewrites every operational subledger document.

For G/L revaluation, inspect source-currency tracking on the relevant balance-sheet account and confirm the posted entries retain the currency information needed by the process. Agree which accounts are in scope, preview the adjustment and compare source-currency amounts with their LCY values. Do not mix this task with additional-reporting-currency translation or a routine customer/vendor adjustment without checking the process documented for each requirement.

Complete the fixed-asset lifecycle

Acquisition establishes the asset cost in the selected depreciation book. Calculate Depreciation proposes entries based on the asset’s method and dates; review the journal before posting. Disposal removes the relevant asset values and records proceeds and the resulting gain or loss using the configured setup. Trace the fixed-asset ledger and integrated G/L entries through all three stages.

In a simplified example, an asset cost 24,000 and has accumulated depreciation of 16,000, so book value is 8,000. Disposal proceeds of 9,500 produce a gain of 1,500 before tax and transaction costs. The proceeds are not the gain. If the disposal journal shows 9,500 income without removing cost and depreciation, investigate the process and posting setup. Complete depreciation to the approved disposal date before evaluating the final book value.

Finish with an evidence-based readiness decision

Use the capstone to connect configuration, document processing, settlement and reconciliation. Review every wrong mock answer by objective and explain why the selected option failed the scenario constraints. Then repeat the relevant practical task with different values. The timed mock score is a learning signal. It is not Microsoft’s scaled score or a guarantee of certification success. A useful readiness decision combines consistent practice performance, successful sandbox evidence and the ability to explain the ledger effects without relying on memorised wording.

Sandbox lab: Perform a miniature period close

Use synthetic records in your authorised sandbox. Record the company and work date before posting. Never run this exercise in a customer’s production company.

  1. Create synthetic invoice/credit/payment examples and calculate the 2,000 less 300 net settlement. Apply entries and verify remaining amounts.
  2. Unapply one deliberate wrong application in the sandbox and reapply it correctly. Confirm that no second cash transaction is created.
  3. Prepare a reconciliation with a 150 unrecorded fee and a separate already-posted unmatched payment. Explain why the required actions differ.
  4. Prepare a recurring 3,000 allocation using 50/30/20 recipients and inspect the 1,500/900/600 result.
  5. Run or preview an authorised exchange-rate adjustment on a small foreign-currency balance. Compare before/after ledger evidence and identify the relevant gain/loss.
  6. Use a disposable G/L dimension-correction example. Then calculate depreciation and a fixed-asset disposal using the 24,000/16,000/9,500 scenario.

Expected observations

  • Settlement/application, bank matching and currency effects reconcile independently.
  • The allocation preserves 3,000 and the simplified asset disposal gain is 1,500.
  • Dimension correction changes the intended analytical classification without being described as changing the original amount.

Evidence to retain

  • Application/unapplication trace
  • Bank reconciliation explanation
  • Allocation and exchange-rate calculations
  • Dimension correction scope
  • Fixed-asset lifecycle ledger reconciliation

Cleanup

Remove unposted training journals, restore test rates/settings when authorised, and retain only redacted synthetic evidence. Keep posted audit trails and follow supported reversal processes.

Common pitfalls

  • Duplicating cash to fix a wrong invoice application.
  • Posting an adjustment for an entry that only needed matching.
  • Treating disposal proceeds as gain, or a mock percentage as Microsoft’s scaled exam score.

Check your understanding

1. An invoice was paid correctly but the payment was applied to the wrong invoice. Is another bank receipt needed?

No. Use the supported unapply and reapply process.

A second receipt would duplicate cash rather than fix the application relationship.

2. Asset cost is 24,000, accumulated depreciation 16,000 and disposal proceeds 9,500. What is the simplified gain?

1,500.

Book value is 8,000, so proceeds exceed book value by 1,500 before tax and other costs.

3. A bank statement line matches an already-posted payment. Should you post it again?

No. Match the statement line to the existing eligible bank ledger entry.

Reconciliation should establish correspondence without duplicating the transaction.
